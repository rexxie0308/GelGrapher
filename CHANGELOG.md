# Changelog

## Unreleased

- **Densitometry on the original pixels.** Each box's outline is mapped back through the rotation and the geometry correction and every original pixel inside it is summed once (`App.sourceRegion`, `quant.js` `regionReader`); the per-row background methods (edge line, border median, rolling ball) work on those rows. No resampled value enters a measurement any more; with a manual 9 % keystone switched on, the Western example's relative values move by at most 1 %. The methods paragraph, the CSV header and the panel note say so. The synthetic Western example's relative values are within 5 % of its truth (the band mode's noise floor on the synthetic's noise; 3 % with the former bilinear read).
- Speed: `ransacModels` builds its ladder models from the twelve strongest peaks and from size pairs spanning at least a third of the ladder with a plausible slope (the search on a 13-lane photograph: 4.3 s → 1.9 s, bench 19/19); `warpGrid` steps each row cell by cell (identical output, twice as fast); the whole load of the 2000 × 1600 photograph takes 5 s.
- Auto reports its steps while it runs (`App.autoGeometry` and `App.autoAll` are async and yield to the browser after each step: slab outline, camera angle round n (+x %), finding the ladders, tracing, straightened); `App.setBright` is async.
- The slab outline's homography is kept only when it scales no cell by more than a fifth (was 0.45). The comb's bands are fitted by least squares over every band, none trimmed, so the standard error is not optimistic; a first-round keystone must move the far lane by at least 0.1 pitch (a 3 % synthetic keystone is found, a 1.5 % one left alone). The legend states the residual lane convergence after the correction and the largest local scale of the map; the panel shows the residual.
- `findPeaks` takes the centre of a plateau (a saturated band), not its first row.
- Tried and withheld: the band rows' vanishing point from the slab's top and bottom edges (`App.slabRowVanishing`, behind `App.slabRowsFromOutline`): on the synthetic Western example the outline's top edge is fitted on the comb teeth and read as a 10 % row convergence on an undistorted gel. A Theil–Sen slope for the comb swung the sanity photograph to +28 % (its spacing changes faster at the bottom than at the top) and was reverted.

- After a supervisor review of the whole pipeline (image recognition, rigour of the re-distortion, marker identification, speed, interface), in this round:
  - Lanes placed by hand ("+ Add lane", a dragged lane line, "Space evenly", "Spread") carry `manual` and are never replaced by the re-detection that follows a red-box drag (the note under the raw image says so); "Re-detect in box" and "Re-detect whole gel" still replace them.
  - The vertical levelling of the ladder band rows needs three ladders that fit well; each row is a straight line through the markers whose scatter must stay under 0.3 of the local row spacing (a mis-assigned band tilts no row), the vertical shift is capped at 0.3 of the row spacing, and the legend states the number of rows and the largest vertical shift separately from the horizontal one.
  - The straightening grid's nodes sit closer than half a lane pitch (up to 129 × 65 over the image instead of 33 × 33), so the field between lanes is realised rather than blurred over a lane; a grid that folds or scales a cell inside the crop by more than a quarter is rejected.
  - The detection polarity (bright bands on dark, or dark on light) is shown under the band-look switch and can be changed by hand (`App.setBright`): the crop, lanes, ladders and geometry are found again with it.
  - A first-round keystone must move the far lane by at least 0.15 pitch over the crop (2.5 % spacing change on 13 lanes); smaller ones are left alone, so a scanner gel never carries a spurious "geometry-corrected" line.
  - The ladder search runs once per load, after the keystone rounds; after a straightening the markers are looked for once more with the gel's ladder as the usual one (cheap). The CV penalty in the ladder-likeness score is capped at 0.2, since ladders with reference bands have a designed CV of 0.5–0.7.
  - The half-pitch guard doubles the comb only when the weak teeth carry no lane of their own (alternate loading keeps every tooth).
  - The well line found by the auto crop follows the frame through a geometry change. The densitometry notes and the CSV header say "rotation and geometry correction in one bilinear resampling" when a correction is on. The panel says "checked · none needed" after an Auto that applied nothing; the range button reads "Loaded only".

- **Lanes are followed as chains of bands.** `E.trackLane` is a Viterbi search: in every horizontal band the candidates are the peaks of the presence profile within 1.2 lane spacings of the lane's lattice position (refined to their centroid), and the chosen chain is the best path under a keep-your-direction prior (the step from one band to the next should repeat the previous step; a missing band costs; a weak pull towards the lattice position keeps the chain out of the neighbour). `E.laneTraces` follows that chain (five-point weighted smoothing applied twice, straight segments between bands, held beyond the first and last band) instead of fitting a quadratic, so a ladder that leans by most of a lane spacing is followed band by band instead of being cut by a vertical line. On the sanity-check photograph the right ladder is now found as a marker (three ladders: lanes 1, 7, 13).
- The straightening is kept only when, besides the median bend falling, the lanes come out better separated in the column density (`App.laneContrast`: the mean presence half-way between neighbouring lanes divided by the mean at the lanes, which falls 0.73 → 0.61 → 0.56 through Auto on the photograph): a warp built from the chains always flattens the chains, so an independent test is needed, and clean gels are no longer straightened on chain jitter.
- The engine self-test's keystone check uses `E.laneComb` (the estimator the app uses) within 3 points.

- The wells and their pockets (down to 1.5 lane spacings below the well line found by the auto crop) are left out of the lane comb and the lane traces: the dark walls of the wells sit between the lanes and offset a comb fitted to those rows by half a lane. The lane detection still reads them (a well is evidence for a lane with no band).
- A comb at half the true pitch (a tooth in every gap between lanes, which smeared gels invite) is recognised by its teeth alternating strong and weak and is doubled onto the strong phase.
- Auto's keystone rounds continue while the residual is significant, keeps its sign and shrinks; a residual that grows, or flips its sign by more than half of the last one, stops them (a deformed slab, not a camera angle).
- `App.autoGeometry.onStep(label)` is called after the slab step, every keystone round and the straightening, so a probe can record the working image and the numbers after every pass (the walkthrough page is built from that).
- Drawing the red box again at a finer scale, or on the region around the first box, was tried and reverted: the auto crop's filters follow the working width (about 700 px), and at another scale they cut faint lanes and short gels. The 700 px working image is a scale normalisation, not a presumption about the photograph; the box is mapped back to full-resolution pixels.

- **Keystone from the lane comb.** The camera angle is now measured by fitting all lanes together as one equally spaced family in every horizontal band of the crop (`E.laneComb`: position and pitch per band, tracked from the middle outward, two parameters over all lanes, so a smear, a torn lane or a sheared well pocket moves no single lane); the pitch's change with height gives the vanishing point. The former per-lane line fit lost lanes that drifted out of their search window and said "no keystone" on the sanity-check photograph; the comb finds +16 % there (the owner's manual box: +16.8 %) and recovers synthetic keystones within one point. Auto now iterates the keystone to convergence first (at most four rounds, each accepted only when it at least halves the remaining convergence) and straightens the lanes once afterwards; the slab outline is kept only when the lanes could be measured before and converge less than half as much after it.
- Lane lattice: a clearly loaded tooth (a ladder) one or two teeth beyond an empty well is still a lane, so a ladder beside a torn or empty well is reached.
- The comb's first fit of every band is robust (median pitch over all pairs of teeth, median offset), so a torn, compressed edge of the slab (two teeth 41 px apart on a 54 px comb) cannot bias the family; on the sanity-check photograph the keystone estimate settles at +12 % (the lanes of the regular part of the comb) where the least-squares fit gave +16 % under the pull of the torn corner.
- Lane width down the gel is measured at each lane's bands (`E.laneTraces`: `w0`, `wTrend`, the linear trend of the band widths over the lane's rows) and reported for lanes whose neighbour widens alike (`E.straightenGrid.widthLanes`); a lane that steadily widens is a local stretch of the gel. The widths are not yet applied to the pixels (`E.straightenGrid.useWidths`): on the photograph the measured trends did not respond to their own correction, which means the lane-wide extent of smeared bands was measured rather than a stretch.
- Marker detection is three times faster on gels with many candidate lanes: the whole ladder library is only tried when no usual ladder fits, and the ladder the lanes agreed on is fitted jointly on its own; the agreed ladder is the one with the largest squared excess fit over 0.55 across lanes, so two lanes fitting one ladder well beat five lysate lanes loosely resembling another.
- The red box re-detects lanes 350 ms after it is released, in the background, so grabbing the next side is never held up; the ladder search only runs when the box gained lanes. A short note under the raw image says when it runs.
- Step 1: "Perspective / distortion" is called **Gel shape**; the sidebar texts are shorter.
- Fixed: a reverted round of Auto left the final report in a stale state object, so the panel could say "bend only" although a keystone had been applied.

### Added
- Straightening judged by the median bend over the trusted lane traces (a trace scattering more than a tenth of the pitch, such as a faint ladder under a smear, is not trusted and its lane follows its neighbours); a round that does not reduce the median bend is reverted, and bends below 5 % of the pitch (3 px) are left alone, so straight gels and the synthetic examples are not touched. On the sanity-check photograph the median bend goes 6.9 → 4.1 → 1.8 px and all three ladders are then recognised with one product.
- Marker lanes: when none of the usual ladders fits well, the ladder is chosen jointly across the candidate lanes (the one with the most clear fits above 0.45), so three ladder lanes of one product beat one lane that fits another product slightly better.
- **Auto geometry starts from the slab outline.** A gel photographed at an angle can drift by a whole lane pitch over its height, which merges neighbouring lanes in the column profile before any lane can be traced. Auto now first finds the four corners of the slab from the gel outline (`E.slabQuad`: the level half-way between the gel and the frame's background, a connected region around the crop opened to cut stains and comb teeth, robust edge lines from the middle 60 % of the region), maps them to a rectangle, and keeps that only when the lane lines come out straighter than before (a torn or shaded outline is dropped); then the recursive lane detection, keystone refinement and lane straightening follow. A synthetic 34 % keystone is recovered to within 0.3 points this way.
- **Manual box with Apply.** Manual shows the blue box with four round corner handles and four square side handles (a side slides along its own perpendicular) and hides every other overlay while it is placed; nothing is mapped until **Apply** (Cancel leaves the image as it was). After Apply the box disappears, the overlays return, and the lanes and markers are detected again.
- The name "M" follows the markers: a lane named M by an earlier detection that is no longer a marker loses the name, so re-detection never leaves stale M labels.
- **Lane straightening, Auto / Manual / Off, and Auto at load.** The geometry correction is now a smooth displacement grid over the image (`E.Grid`) that holds the perspective and a per-lane straightening in one resampling. **Auto** runs when an image is opened and on the button: in every round the keystone is estimated from the lane lines (and the ladder band rows) while no straightening has been applied yet, then every lane is traced down the crop (centroids tracked band by band, a quadratic per lane, held beyond the rows where the lane was seen) and the traces are made straight and vertical; the ladder band rows across markers that fit well are levelled too; sample bands are never anchors; the lanes are detected again on the corrected image and the round repeats until they settle (at most five). **Manual** shows a box with four free corners on the raw image: drag each onto a corner of the gel and the gel inside is mapped to a rectangle of the same centre and mean side lengths (one projective transform). **Off** returns to the uncorrected image. Lanes and markers are detected again after the crop is dragged, after Auto settles, after a corner is released and after Off, keeping names by position and any ladder chosen by hand. Everything is stated in the legend notes (perspective, largest lane shift, the number of traces and ladder rows), the log (with the grid) and the methods paragraph.
- Marker lanes: one ladder per gel. The lane that looks most like a ladder names it (the usual ladders when one of them fits a lane well, otherwise the whole library), and the other candidate lanes are kept only when they fit that ladder about as well; a lane that fits poorly next to one that fits well is dropped. A gel run with a ladder the user has not used before now finds its marker lanes (the sanity-check photograph: both visible ladders, NEB P7717, where one lysate lane or none was picked before); the 19-variant marker bench stays exactly right.
- **Auto perspective is recursive.** A distorted gel often yields only some of its lanes at first: after each correction the lanes are detected again on the corrected image (names stay by position), the perspective is estimated again, and this repeats until the lane count and positions stop changing and no significant convergence is left (at most five rounds); the markers are then found again, keeping any ladder chosen by hand. The message says how many rounds and how many wells.
- **Perspective (keystone) correction** (step 1, *Straighten and contrast* → *Perspective*). A gel photographed at an angle has converging lanes; **Auto** measures the convergence of the lane lines (and of the ladder bands when two or more markers are matched), finds their vanishing point and corrects it with one projective transform of the whole image, repeated on the corrected image until the lanes no longer converge; it refuses when the lanes do not converge *consistently* (a gel distorted by itself, smears, a torn corner), since that is not perspective. Typed percentages (lane spacing at the bottom of the box relative to the top, band spacing right relative to left) build a symmetric keystone by hand; **Off** returns to the uncorrected image. The transform is composed with the straightening so the pixels are resampled once (bilinear, no upsampling); every annotation is carried through it and back; it follows a later rotation and undo; it is stated in the legend notes, the processing log (with the matrix), the methods paragraph and the raster summary; off by default and never applied silently. Design, rules and literature: `docs/geometry-correction.md`.
- **Lane detection rebuilt for photographed gels.** The lane pitch comes from a comb fit of the lane-presence profile (the sum at the comb's teeth minus the sum between them, measured inside the crop), not from the gaps between the peaks it happened to find, so it no longer doubles when weak lanes sit between strong ones; every analysis parameter scales with the crop (the gel), not with the frame the gel was photographed in; the presence profile adds the 75th and 50th row percentiles, which separate stained lanes (lysates, Coomassie) that touch, and a small horizontal opening first, which drops the slanted cut edge of a gel, folds and dust; the gel slab is the first strong step down to the background outward from the loaded lanes; a well template found on background (a photograph with the "well row" above the slab) is rejected unless the loaded lanes agree with it; teeth in the gel margin are not wells. On a photographed SDS-PAGE with three ladders, a torn corner and keystone the app now finds its 13 wells (before: 14 at twice the pitch, mostly beside the gel); the 19 synthetic marker-bench variants are all exactly right (three lost lanes before). `node tools/lane-bench.mjs` runs the detector on the synthetic gels (and on a raw 8-bit image with `--raw`).
- **+ Add lane** (step 2): click it, then click on the raw image where the missing lane runs; the blue line follows the pointer, Shift+click adds several, Esc ends. Double-clicking empty space still works.
- Several lane lines at once: Shift+click (or Ctrl+click) adds a line to the selection on the raw image; a drag or the arrow keys move every selected line together, Delete removes them all. "Spread between the two selected" (step 2, "Lanes detected wrongly?") spaces the lanes between two selected lines evenly, so a run of lanes is fixed by placing its first and last line (idea from BlotFigure).
- A typed target-band size moves the arrow: entering "35 kDa" or "1.5 kb" in the arrow window puts the arrow where the markers place that size, and the magenta line on the image follows; dragging the arrow afterwards goes back to the measured size.
- NEB ladders are listed with their catalogue numbers including the pack-size suffix (P7719S / P7719V, N3232S / N3232L …), so a search for the number on the tube finds them.
- **Analysis window.** The right column holds two windows: the figure and, under it, the analysis (chart, table, lane profile, band sizes). "Figure" and "Analysis" in the top bar show or hide each, every window has a × in its header, a divider between them sets their share (remembered), and when both are closed two buttons bring them back. The last window clicked is the active one, marked by an accent bar, and decides which overlay layer the raw image edits (strips with the figure, band boxes with the analysis). "+ Band box" opens the analysis window; a gel with band boxes opens with both. Each window has its own export control in its header: "Export figure" (PNG, TIFF, PDF, SVG, PowerPoint) and "Export chart" (the same formats). The plot options (bars or points, written values, reference line, title, y-axis label) and the methods text are folds of step 7.
- **Export all.** The green button at the top right now saves everything in one ZIP (figure as PNG, PDF and SVG; chart as PNG and SVG; measurements and band sizes as CSV; processing log; methods text); its arrow still lists the figure alone in every format, copying, printing, the uncropped image, the original data and the log.
- Densitometry: **Median of the box border** as a background method (a 3-pixel border around the box, the way Image Studio and Image Lab do it) beside the edge line and the rolling ball; **Normalize to** total lane density (total-protein normalization on a stain-free or stained gel) or nothing, beside the loading-control box. The CSV, the processing log, the legend notes and a new methods paragraph (Export step, "Copy") state what was done.
- Twelve protein ladders, each read from the vendor's product page (sources in `docs/ladder-sources.md`): Thermo PageRuler Unstained Low Range (26632), Spectra Multicolor High Range (26625) and Low Range (26628); Epizyme WJ101, WJ102, WJ103, WJ106, WJ107 and WJ402; Yeasen GoldBand Plus 3-color 10–180 kDa (26616ES), 8–180 kDa (20350ES) and GoldBand 3-color High Range 10–245 kDa (20352ES).
- PowerPoint export (export menu → "PowerPoint (editable)"): one slide the size of the figure in which every label is a text box and every line, frame and arrow head a native shape, so they can be edited in PowerPoint; the gel is one picture per shown block (lane segment × strip), each a native crop of the whole processed crop, so dragging the crop handles in PowerPoint reveals more of the gel. Fonts are referenced by name, not embedded. Dependency-free, works inside the Claude artifact. Approach after GelFigure 0.6.7.
- Raster exports are checked against the browser's canvas limit before anything is drawn. Safari, and every browser on iPhone and iPad, blank out a canvas above about 16.7 million pixels, which a double-column figure reaches at 1200 dpi; the Layout step now shows the pixel size of the export, the pre-flight check offers the largest resolution that fits, and "Export PNG" says so instead of saving a blank image. TIFFs are drawn in horizontal bands, so they are not bound by that limit.
- Typeface (step 6, Layout): Arial (default), Helvetica or Times New Roman for every text of the figure; installed system fonts only, nothing is downloaded or embedded. The same choice drives the text measurement, the SVG, the PDF (Times New Roman is set in Times; Arial and Helvetica share metrics) and style templates; an unknown name falls back to Arial.
- Lane-name angle: "Custom" with a 0–90° field beside the presets; the value is kept in projects and styles.
- Palette (indexed-colour) TIFFs load, as do 1-, 2- and 4-bit TIFFs (ImageJ 8-bit saves, scanned gels, false-colour imager exports): a grey colour map gives the grey values (inverted maps included), a false-colour map whose brightness runs one way is read back as the saved intensity index, and any other map is converted by luminance; a note says which happened, and a 4-bit file warns that it has only 16 grey levels. Approach after GelFigure 0.6.6.
- Long group labels (step 6, Layout): a label wider than its group line is now **wrapped** onto even lines (default), or **shrunk** (not below 5 pt), or kept on **one line**; a label wider than its line moves the groups above it up a tier instead of colliding with them, and the pre-flight check notices a shrunk label. Shift+Enter in the label box starts a new line by hand. Approach after GelFigure 0.6.5.
- The Densitometry view switch no longer carries a count of band boxes.
- Hands-on tutorial: on the first visit the app opens on the empty workspace and a tutorial makes a figure with you, one real action at a time, in sixteen steps: open a practice gel, the three areas, band look (bright or dark), straightening, contrast, the figure area, checking and removing lanes, naming, the ladder, group line, target band, condition row, strips, densitometry with a loading control, export. Each step is one card: it says what the area is for and walks through the task, and the spotlight moves from the button to the figure or the next control as you go (the fold a control sits in is opened for you and stays lit); button names appear as key caps in the text; while you work in the settings panel the card sits at its bottom left; the rest of the screen is dimmed while the control and the area where the result shows (the raw image, the figure) stay lit; the card says "Done" when the app shows the action was done, no text box, window or dialog is open, and a label that was asked for has been typed, and then waits for Next (nothing moves on by itself); any step can be skipped; the spotlight follows the panel when it scrolls; notices wait for the contrast step and are checked again before the export, and the practice gel is made so that the figure passes the pre-flight check. Replay it on any gel, your own included, with "Guide me" in the top bar or ? → "Hands-on tutorial".
- While a target band is being placed, a dashed line on the figure follows the mouse, snapped to the band under it, with the estimated size, so you see where the arrow will go before clicking.
- The text box for a strip label opens to the right of the gel and the one for a condition name to the left of it, and the target-band window opens beside the figure, so none of them covers the gel image.
- On the raw image, a named lane shows its name in a light pill above the well-number chip instead of "2 · name" in one chip.
- "Group lines break at splice gaps" (step 6, Layout). Off by default: a group line now runs on across a splice gap, since the gap itself shows that a lane was removed; tick it to break the line there as before.

The figure column follows the step you work in: touching any control of steps 1–6 (a tool such as "Target band", "Group line", "Condition row" or "Strip", a lane, a ladder band, a layout option) brings the figure back, and any control of step 7 (or "+ Band box") opens the Densitometry view; the view switch flashes so the change is seen.

### Fixed
- A condition row no longer has a cell under the ladder lane.
- Densitometry: the chart plotted values relative to the reference lane even when that lane had no band in a box, which divided by background noise and made the real bands vanish next to values in the thousands. A relative value is now given only when the reference lane has a real band in that box (at least 5 % of the strongest lane); otherwise the table shows "–" for it, the chart shows the values themselves, and a note asks for another reference lane.

## 1.1.0 (2026-10-21)

### Changed
- Dependencies: Playwright 1.64.1; GitHub Actions checkout v7, setup-node v7, configure-pages v6, upload-pages-artifact v5, deploy-pages v5. The test runner falls back to a preinstalled Chromium when the bundled one is missing.
- One palette with Paper Radar: cool mist ground, slate ink, teal for anything you can act on, amber only for warnings, green only for "fine"; a light top bar; the same tokens in dark mode. The figure stays black on white and the overlay colours on the raw image are unchanged.
- The panel's first screen holds the daily steps (experiment, band look, lanes, names, marker): rotation, flips, contrast and gamma sit under "Straighten and contrast", which states what was set automatically, and the lane-fixing tools under "Lanes detected wrongly?". Hints are shorter.
- English-only interface: the Chinese translation layer (`js/i18n.js`, `L()` calls, `data-en` attributes) and the language button are removed.

### Added
- Resizable layout: drag the divider between the settings panel and the stage, or the one between the raw image and the figure (left/right, or top/bottom when they are stacked). Sizes are remembered; double-click a divider to reset it; arrow keys move a focused divider.
- Installable app: the web version registers a service worker (`sw.js`) that stores the app in the browser, so it opens offline; each deployment is stamped with its commit and the next online start offers "Reload" to switch to it. "Install as an app" in the File menu where the browser supports it; web app manifest and icons. Not part of the Claude artifact or of a locally opened file.
- Gels with several rows of wells (two combs): the rows are found, one row is shown at a time with its own crop, lanes and ladder, and a row switcher under "Lanes in figure" keeps the names and annotations of every row.
- Lane chips on the raw image thin out when many lanes are shown at a small zoom instead of piling up.
- Flip vertical / horizontal (Image section): for gels imaged with the wells at the bottom or mirrored. Every annotation follows, the processing log and legend notes record the orientation change.
- "Copy figure to clipboard" in the export menu: a PNG at the chosen dpi for slides and notebooks.
- Arrow labels on the same side of the gel no longer overlap: close ones are spread apart with a short leader line.

### Fixed
- The favourite-ladder list and the custom-ladder unit followed the experiment chosen at start-up, not the loaded gel's (DNA ladders listed on a Western).
- The marker's band list closed after every tick; it now stays open, at the same scroll position, so several bands can be shown or hidden in a row.
- Checkboxes placed directly in a field (saturated pixels, lane numbers, frame, auto width, gamma unlock) lost their layout.
- Lane chips on the raw image read "2 0 h" for a lane named "0 h"; now "2 · 0 h".
- The zoom percentage was empty until the first wheel zoom; the "Raw image" column title wrapped; the panel-letter box stretched across the panel.
- The synthetic examples are no longer autosaved as drafts, so a later visit opens the app instead of offering an "unfinished" example.
- Unreadable files say which file failed and which formats are supported.
- Discarding the autosaved draft could be undone by an autosave that was already due; the discard now cancels it.

## 1.0.0 (2026-10-19)

First GelGrapher release, built on GelFigure 0.5.0 (MIT).

### Added
- Experiment types: DNA agarose gel, DNA PAGE, Southern blot, RNA gel / Northern blot, protein SDS-PAGE, native / Blue native PAGE and Western blot. Each type has its own ladder library, size unit (bp, nt, kDa) and default band look.
- Ladder library: 18 protein ladders (Thermo PageRuler / Spectra, Bio-Rad Precision Plus, NEB, Invitrogen Novex Sharp, SeeBlue Plus2, MagicMark XP, BenchMark), 2 native markers, 5 RNA ladders and 4 more DNA ladders (50 in total) (NEB Low Molecular Weight, Invitrogen 1 Kb Plus, HyperLadder 1kb, Lambda/HindIII).
- Densitometry: band boxes, three background methods, normalization to a loading control and a reference lane, saturation flags, lane profile plot, CSV and publication bar chart (PNG / SVG / PDF).
- Band-size table for every lane, with CSV export.
- Strips: show only chosen regions of a blot, stacked with a gap and labelled (target + loading control), disclosed in the legend notes.
- Western marker image: a white-light / colorimetric image of the same membrane can be loaded to find the ladder bands; it travels with projects and drafts.
- Vector PDF export (standard fonts, Greek letters via the Symbol font, gel embedded losslessly).
- Claude artifact build (`npm run build`): exports go through the viewer's save prompt (TIFF and projects inside a .zip), and "Draft legend and methods with Claude" writes a legend and a methods paragraph from a text summary of the figure.
- Worked examples (synthetic DNA gel, Coomassie SDS-PAGE, Western blot); the Western example opens on first visit.
- Dark theme, phone layout, touch and pen input with pinch zoom, in-page dialogs.

### Changed
- Automatic crop no longer stops at a run of faint lanes, so protein gels with wash / flow-through lanes keep their marker and lysate lanes.
- Marker lanes are chosen by how well they fit a ladder, not only by how many bands they have, so lysate lanes are not mistaken for ladders.
