# Changelog

## Unreleased

- **Densitometry, calculation version 2: why a broad band read low, and the fix.** The earlier default took each lane's background from the first and last two rows *inside* the box (a line between them) and cut negative rows at zero. A band that reaches those rows (a broad or smeared band, or a box drawn tight) has part of itself subtracted: on a synthetic blot whose lane 4 band holds 2.9 × lane 2's protein in a band three times as tall, that background read lane 4 at **0.74 ×** lane 2, the lowest lane, with no warning. Values are now the **signed sum of signal minus background** over the region, with the background taken from **regions outside it** (above and below by default, 2 px away, 40 % of the region's height deep; above only, below only or beside; per lane if needed), drawn dashed on the image. The same blot reads 1.67 × with the band's region unchanged (and is marked "? Needs review": the band reaches the region's edges and the background regions rise towards it), and **2.68 ×** (truth 2.89 ×, the highest lane, no flag) once the region is as tall as the band. Nothing is done to a lane's number: the region and the background are corrected for every lane. On a noiseless test with regions that hold the bands, the values equal the true integrated amounts to 0.01 %.
- **Signed, never clamped.** Blank lanes read about zero, about half of them below: mean −0.08 (sd 0.55) over 30 blank lanes, against +0.60 (none below zero) with the clamping. U − B = corrected is shown for every lane (uncorrected sum, background contribution, signal and background pixel counts), in the profile, the CSV and the processing log.
- **Regions are fixed in the original pixels.** A band's regions are placed once, on the image as shown, and mapped to the decoded file's pixels; rotating, flipping, correcting the perspective, changing the figure box, hiding or reordering lanes, saving and reopening, undo and redo no longer move them (before, a 3° rotation changed one lane's value from 172 to 10). Every original pixel whose centre lies inside a region is counted once with weight 1; regions that share an edge share its pixels exactly (`Engine.rasterPoly`, `boxPixels`, `frameMap`). Only an explicit edit places a band again (dragging its edges, its Height, Re-centre on lanes, the region width), counted in the provenance. Lanes have stable ids, so a region and the reference lane stay with their lane when lanes are re-detected, added or removed.
- **Flags instead of silent errors.** Each lane is checked for a band reaching the region's top or bottom, background regions rising towards the band (they hold part of it), a background region that is not flat (another band or a smear), two peaks in the region, a background region over another measured band, pixels off the image, and how much the value moves when the region is made taller or the background moved away. Any of these marks the lane "? Needs review" with the reasons, in the table, the chart, the CSV, the clipboard and the log; a ratio whose divisor or reference lane needs review is marked too. Clipped pixels are counted at the file's digital limit or a pile-up below it; clipped values are hatched in the chart, never used as a divisor or reference. A value that cannot be given says why ("Choose a reference lane", "Reference band (GAPDH) below its 10σ noise threshold in this lane") and is drawn "n/a", never as a zero bar; negative values go below the zero line.
- **Noise thresholds, stated as estimates.** 3σ and 10σ of the region's sum, σ from the median absolute deviation of the background pixels, allowing for correlated neighbouring pixels and for the uncertainty of the background level. They are called estimated noise thresholds, not assay limits; where the background regions are not flat no threshold call is made.
- **One output, chosen.** Output is **Background-corrected signal**, **Normalized signal** or **Fold change vs a reference lane you choose**; normalization is **None — background-corrected signal**, a **Reference band** (named) or **Total lane signal** (total-protein normalization only when you tick that the image is a total-protein stain or stain-free image). Nothing is chosen for you and the output never switches by itself. The table, plot, profile, clipboard (with units, flags and the reference), CSV (with every region's corners in original pixels), methods text, processing log and the summary for Claude all come from one result.
- **Earlier projects keep their numbers.** A project measured with the earlier calculation opens as version 1, says so ("Earlier method") and offers **Update to the current method…**, which asks first and can be undone; adding a band to it asks too. Lane ids are added on opening.
- **A compact densitometry step.** "+ Add band"; a band table with its Height, the lanes it covers, Inspect and a ⋯ menu (Re-centre on lanes, Place missing lanes, Use as reference band, Delete); one line per setting (Measure: Whole region / Thresholded (exploratory); Background with Edit…; Normalize; Output; Measured lanes with the reasons a lane is not measured; Analysis area with Edit… and Reset from figure…); Method details hold the methods paragraph. The profile shows one lane's signal and the exact background rows it used, or all lanes on a common scale, with a readout of U − B = value, the regions' corners in original pixels and the sensitivity checks; "Adjust this lane" moves its edges or its background (recorded as a lane-specific region). Plot settings sit beside the plot; clicking a bar or a table row selects the lane everywhere.

- **Densitometry is linear by default, and every value carries its limits.** The default measure was the thresholded one (only pixels above the background noise floor counted), which drops the faint part of every band: in a noiseless test doubling a band read as 3.95 times, and on a two-fold dilution series its log-log slope is 1.1–1.3 with the faintest lanes reading zero (`tools/quant-bench.mjs`). The default is now the **whole box** (every pixel after background subtraction; slope 0.98 with the edge background); the thresholded measure stays as "Thresholded signal", with a warning, and its claim of box-size independence is gone. Every box has a limit of detection and of quantification (3 and 10 σ of its total, σ from the noise of its edge rows and the pixels its background comes from); a value below its LOQ is marked (‹LOQ in the table, † on relative values, a dashed bar in the chart) and is never used as a divisor or as the reference. Projects saved before keep their measure.
- **The analysis has its own area and lanes.** The analysis area is set from the figure box when the first band box is drawn and kept apart from it (dashed green on the image when they differ; "Use the figure box" sets it again); the lanes measured are the lanes in it, untick one under "Lanes measured" to leave it out. Narrowing the red box or leaving a lane out of the figure no longer changes a single number (the lane signal used for normalization was taken over the figure box, and hidden lanes were dropped from the analysis). Projects saved before keep their results: their analysis area is their figure box and their hidden lanes stay out of the analysis.
- **Normalization is a deliberate choice.** Nothing is normalized until you choose; a second band box is no longer made the loading control by itself. "Divide by this box" on a box chooses loading-control normalization, and the hint says it holds only for a band that does not change between conditions and is within its linear range. "Lane signal" is total-protein normalization only when you tick that the image is a total-protein stain or stain-free image; otherwise the methods text says it is the lane signal of this image. A normalized value whose divisor is clipped or below its LOQ is not given, and the table says why. The divisor's own values (the loading-control box, or the lane signal) are in the table.
- **Saturation is read where it happens.** The check counts original pixels (not the resampled working image) at the band end of the scale: the top for bright bands, the bottom for dark bands (Coomassie, silver, film), which were never checked. The level is where this file clips: its digital limit, or a pile-up below it (12-bit data in a 16-bit file pile up at 4095, which the old 0.998 threshold never reached). The report says which of three things it is: pixels at the digital limit, a probable clip (pile-up), or nothing found, and always that detector saturation before the image was written is not recorded in a file. Floating-point data say "cannot tell"; JPEG adds that compression can hide a clip; a colour image is converted to grey by luminance with a warning that a multi-channel composite cannot be quantified per channel.
- **The marker image shares the blot's geometry.** It was rotated only, so under a perspective or straightening correction it sat in a different frame from the blot. It now goes through the same rotation and geometry grid; its offset from the blot is measured when it is loaded (gradient cross-correlation; equal pixel size is not proof of alignment) and stated under the marker image, with an opacity slider for the overlay. Switching to another exposure of the same size measures the offset too and warns when there is one.
- **One description of the method.** The analysis window, the CSV header, the methods paragraph, the legend notes, the processing log and the summary sent to Claude are written from the same sentences (`G.Quant.describe`), so they cannot disagree; the CSV and the log carry each value's LOQ and flags. The rolling-ball background's limits count its baseline as set by a few rows (its opening sits low on a noisy profile and reads faint bands too high; the bench shows it).

- Flip vertical / horizontal (Image section): for gels imaged with the wells at the bottom or mirrored. Every annotation follows, the processing log and legend notes record the orientation change.
- "Copy figure to clipboard" in the export menu: a PNG at the chosen dpi for slides and notebooks.
- Arrow labels on the same side of the gel no longer overlap: close ones are spread apart with a short leader line.

### Fixed
- The favourite-ladder list and the custom-ladder unit followed the experiment chosen at start-up, not the loaded gel's (DNA ladders listed on a Western).
- The marker's band list closed after every tick; it now stays open, at the same scroll position, so several bands can be shown or hidden in a row.
- Checkboxes placed directly in a field (saturated pixels, lane numbers, frame, auto width, gamma unlock) lost their layout.
- Lane chips on the raw image read "2 0 h" for a lane named "0 h"; now "2 · 0 h".
- The zoom percentage was empty until the first wheel zoom; the "Raw image" column title wrapped; the panel-letter box stretched across the panel.

## 1.0.0 (2026-10-19)

First GelGrapher release, built on GelFigure 0.5.0 (MIT).

### Added
- Experiment types: DNA agarose gel, DNA PAGE, Southern blot, RNA gel / Northern blot, protein SDS-PAGE, native / Blue native PAGE and Western blot. Each type has its own ladder library, size unit (bp, nt, kDa) and default band look.
- Ladder library: 18 protein ladders (Thermo PageRuler / Spectra, Bio-Rad Precision Plus, NEB, Invitrogen Novex Sharp, SeeBlue Plus2, MagicMark XP, BenchMark), 2 native markers, 5 RNA ladders and 4 more DNA ladders (50 in total) (NEB Low Molecular Weight, Invitrogen 1 Kb Plus, HyperLadder 1kb, Lambda/HindIII).
- Densitometry: band boxes, three background methods, normalization to a loading control and a reference lane, saturation flags, lane profile plot, CSV and publication bar chart (PNG / SVG / PDF).
- Band-size table for every lane, with CSV export.
- Strips: show only chosen regions of a blot, stacked with a gap and labelled (target + loading control), disclosed in the legend notes.
- Western marker image: a white-light / colorimetric image of the same membrane can be loaded to find the ladder bands; it travels with projects and drafts.
- Vector PDF export (standard fonts, Greek letters via the Symbol font, gel embedded losslessly).
- Claude artifact build (`npm run build`): exports go through the viewer's save prompt (TIFF and projects inside a .zip), and "Draft legend and methods with Claude" writes a legend and a methods paragraph from a text summary of the figure.
- Worked examples (synthetic DNA gel, Coomassie SDS-PAGE, Western blot); the Western example opens on first visit.
- Dark theme, phone layout, touch and pen input with pinch zoom, in-page dialogs.

### Changed
- Automatic crop no longer stops at a run of faint lanes, so protein gels with wash / flow-through lanes keep their marker and lysate lanes.
- Marker lanes are chosen by how well they fit a ladder, not only by how many bands they have, so lysate lanes are not mistaken for ladders.
